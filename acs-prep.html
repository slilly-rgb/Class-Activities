1 · The ACS spectrum about 8 min

Coronary artery disease narrows arteries with plaque. A stable plaque causes predictable pain when demand rises. When a plaque ruptures, a clot forms on it. That is acute coronary syndrome, and how completely the clot blocks the artery decides which kind.

Who presents atypically: women, older adults and people with diabetes often report fatigue, shortness of breath, nausea, or jaw, back or upper-abdominal discomfort instead of crushing chest pain. Treat these as possible ACS until proven otherwise.

Risk factors: you can change smoking, hypertension, high LDL, diabetes, obesity and inactivity. You can't change age, male sex, family history of early heart disease, or menopause.

2 · Emergency care about 10 min

The first 10 minutes

  • 12-lead ECG within 10 minutes of arrival. The ECG, not the troponin, decides whether this is a STEMI.
  • Aspirin 162–325 mg, chewed and not enteric-coated, unless the client is allergic or actively bleeding.
  • Nitroglycerin 0.4 mg SL every 5 minutes, up to 3 doses. Hold for SBP under 90, HR under 50, a PDE5 inhibitor (sildenafil or vardenafil within 24 hours, tadalafil within 48 hours), or suspected right ventricular infarction.
  • Oxygen only if SpO₂ is under 90%.
  • IV access, troponin and other labs, continuous cardiac monitoring. Morphine only for pain that nitroglycerin doesn't relieve.

Signs the artery has opened: pain eases, ST elevation resolves, and brief reperfusion dysrhythmias may appear.

Worked example · read it step by step

Mr. James Whitaker, 63, has crushing chest pain. The ECG shows ST elevation in the inferior leads. BP 88/56, HR 52. Lungs are clear, but JVD is present at 45°. He asks for "one of those under-the-tongue pills."

  1. Name it. ST elevation means STEMI, so the clock starts: activate the cath lab now.
  2. Think about the artery. The inferior wall is usually fed by the right coronary artery, which also supplies the SA and AV nodes and the right ventricle.
  3. Explain the findings. HR 52 fits RCA involvement of the conduction system. Hypotension with JVD but clear lungs points to a right ventricular infarction: the right side can't push blood forward, so it backs up into the veins, not the lungs.
  4. Spot the danger. A failing right ventricle depends on preload. Nitroglycerin, morphine and diuretics lower preload and can crash his pressure, and his SBP is already under 90.
  5. Decide the priority. Hold the nitroglycerin and tell him why. Give aspirin and IV fluid boluses as ordered, keep atropine or pacing ready for bradycardia, and get him to the cath lab.

CABG bypasses blocked arteries with a vein or artery graft when disease is widespread. Early care focuses on bleeding from chest tubes, tamponade, dysrhythmias, BP control, warming and pain control so the client can cough and deep breathe.

Discharge teaching

  • Chest pain at home: stop and sit, take 1 nitroglycerin SL. If the pain isn't better or is worse 5 minutes later, call 911. Keep tablets in their original dark container.
  • After a stent: take dual antiplatelet therapy exactly as ordered. Stopping early can make the stent clot. Tell every dentist and provider about it.
  • No PDE5 inhibitors (sildenafil, tadalafil) with nitrates. Sexual activity can usually resume when the client can climb 2 flights of stairs without symptoms.
  • Cardiac rehab, smoking cessation, a heart-healthy diet, and BP, glucose and lipid control.

Time goals and drug lists vary slightly by textbook. On exams, use your course text.

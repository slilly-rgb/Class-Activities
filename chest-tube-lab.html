Learn how a chest tube restores the lung, what each chamber of the drainage unit does, and what to do when it looks wrong. Then prove it.

Why the lung needs a chest tube

NORMAL

Negative pressure

The pleural space holds a thin film of fluid at pressure slightly below atmospheric. That vacuum keeps the lung stuck to the chest wall so it expands with every breath.

PROBLEM

Air or fluid gets in

Pneumothorax (air), hemothorax (blood), pleural effusion or empyema break the vacuum. The lung pulls away from the chest wall and collapses.

FIX

Drain it one way

The tube lets air and fluid leave but never return. As the space empties, negative pressure comes back and the lung re-expands.

Air: high and anteriorAir rises, so a tube for pneumothorax usually sits near the apex, about the 2nd intercostal space, midclavicular line.
Fluid: low and lateralFluid settles, so a tube for blood or effusion usually sits around the 4th–6th intercostal space, mid-axillary line.
Mediastinal tubesAfter cardiac surgery, tubes drain blood from around the heart. They are not in the pleural space.
At the bedsideSterile water, a sterile occlusive dressing (petroleum gauze per policy) and padded clamps for emergencies only.

Inside the drainage unit

Tap a chamber. The unit is "breathing" with a spontaneously breathing patient, so watch the water seal rise and fall.

Rules that keep the system working

Below the chest, uprightGravity drains the chest. A unit above chest level can send drainage back in; a tipped unit loses its water seal.
No dependent loopsCoil extra tubing flat on the bed. A loop hanging below fills with fluid and blocks drainage.
Don't clamp routinelyClamping traps air in the chest and can cause a tension pneumothorax. Clamp only briefly to locate a leak or change the unit, per policy.
Don't strip or milk routinelyStripping creates very high negative pressure that can injure tissue. Follow provider order and policy.
Disconnected from the unitPut the patient end of the tube 2–3 cm into sterile water to restore a seal, then replace the unit.
Pulled out of the chestCover the site with a sterile occlusive dressing taped on three sides, so air can escape but not enter. Call the provider.
